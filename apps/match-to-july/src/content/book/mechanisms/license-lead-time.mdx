## Scope

Training licenses go by many names (permit, limited license, registration, postgraduate training license) and each state sets its own rule. As of 2026-10-21, Andwise's state data lists [42 of 51 jurisdictions (50 states and DC)](https://www.joinandwise.com/residency) as requiring one, 5 where it depends on the program or the training year, and 4 (Arkansas, Maryland, New York and Utah) with a statutory or board exemption for residents in accredited programs. Each state's page links the board's own source.

Published lead times, quoted from the boards:

- **Texas.** Board rule 22 TAC 161.55: apply at least 60 and no more than 120 days before the program starts; the board suggests allowing 90 to 120 days ([Texas Medical Board](https://www.tmb.texas.gov/apply-renew/physician/physician-apply/physician-in-training-permit-application); [Andwise Texas page](https://www.joinandwise.com/residency/texas)).
- **District of Columbia.** Regulation: submit no later than 90 days before starting clinical training ([DC Health](https://dchealth.dc.gov/service/board-medicine-medical-training-license-and-registrant-application)).
- **Florida.** Application and fee at least 60 days before the start date; training may not begin until a registration number is issued ([Florida Board of Medicine](https://flboardofmedicine.gov/resident-physicians-interns-fellows-and-house-physicians/)).
- **Illinois.** Department rule: file at least 60 days before training starts ([68 Ill. Adm. Code 1285](https://ilga.gov/ftp/JCAR/AdminCode/068/068012850A00900R.html)).
- **California.** No apply-by date, but the Postgraduate Training License must be held within 180 days of starting ([Medical Board of California](https://www.mbc.ca.gov/Licensing/Postgraduate-Training-Licensees/Apply.aspx)).
- **Kentucky.** First-year residents are exempt; the PGY-2 license takes about 6 to 8 weeks, and training verification is not accepted before April 1 ([Kentucky Board of Medical Licensure](https://kbml.ky.gov/physician/Pages/Types-of-Licenses.aspx)).

Of the 51 entries, 20 carry a published lead time or processing time (18 that require a license, plus Kentucky and Washington, where it varies), 27 require or may require a license and publish no lead time (24 and 3), and the 4 exempt jurisdictions have no license to time: 20 + 27 + 4 = 51. Fees run from $10 (New Mexico, plus screening fees) to $674 (California's PTL, including fingerprinting).

## Boundaries

- The mechanism is the board's clock. A late document from the medical school or a background check is a cause inside that clock, not a separate mechanism.
- State detail belongs on the state pages at `joinandwise.com/residency/<state>`, which are checked against each board; this book summarizes the pattern only.
