## Ownership

Every alert needs a single named owner before anyone does anything else. The owner can change, but the handoff must be explicit and recorded.

## Trigger

You are in this runbook when the pager fires, when a handoff arrives without a repro, or when a deploy is about to start without a rollback candidate.

## Steps

- Classify the alert and name an owner.
- Check the rollback plan before the deploy starts.
- Capture a reproduction before the diagnosis moves.
- Escalate to a named target when the window is close to firing.

## Escalation

Escalation is bounded: a target, a trigger, and a window. When the window is close, the owner ramps to the named target and records the transfer.

## Verification

The runbook works when an alert becomes a classified severity, a named owner, a written repro, and a verified recovery — measured by the drills.

## Reader habits to use right away

- Follow the load: when an incident appears, ask who owns it and what the rollback plan is.
- Write the repro line before you debug: "trigger → expected → observed."
- Pace the escalation: a sev-1 that stalls past its window is a procedure failure, not a willpower failure.
