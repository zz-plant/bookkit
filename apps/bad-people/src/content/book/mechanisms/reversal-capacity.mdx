slug: reversal-capacity
status: draft
title: "Reversal Capacity"
definition: "The ability to reverse harm depends on access to documentation, allies, and buffers, not on who is right."
is:
  - "A structural advantage for people with resources to contest decisions."
  - "A gap between formal rights and practical ability to use them."
isNot:
  - "A promise that the system is fair because appeals exist."
  - "A personal trait like courage or resilience."
predictions:
  - "People with allies or documentation are more likely to get reversals."
  - "Those without buffers accept harm because reversal is too costly."
  - "Processes that claim to be open still require insider knowledge to navigate."
tests:
  - "Map recourse cost across time, money, repetition, and vigilance; if total cost exceeds the harm, reversal capacity is low."
  - "Compare outcomes for similar cases with and without documentation."
---
