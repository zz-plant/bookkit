## Claim

When cooling is underfunded, people become the thermal mass that keeps systems operational.

This is not metaphor-first rhetoric; it is a load-allocation description. Systems keep throughput by routing volatility into human waiting, administrative labor, vigilance, and stress physiology.

## The extraction test

A system passes the extraction test only if it can answer all four checks affirmatively:

1. **Buffer check:** Is there funded surge capacity for predictable spikes?
2. **Rollback check:** Can harmful states be reversed at roughly the speed they are imposed?
3. **Ownership check:** Is a named owner accountable for repair, not only review?
4. **Recourse check:** Can people contest outcomes without additional heroic labor?

Failing two or more checks means resilience is being purchased by externalized human absorption.

## Observable indicators

- Queue inflation concentrates at points requiring human interpretation.
- "Self-advocacy" guidance rises while staffing and authority remain static.
- Delayed cases resolve through withdrawal more often than merits correction.
- Frontline apology labor increases faster than formal repair capacity.

## What changes design decisions

Treat heat injuries as operational failures, not user communication issues. If the same person repeatedly carries overflow, the system has selected a hidden infrastructure layer and should be redesigned accordingly.
