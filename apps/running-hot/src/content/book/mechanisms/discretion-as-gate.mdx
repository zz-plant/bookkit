---
slug: discretion-as-gate
status: draft
title: "Discretion as gate"
definition: "A decision architecture where opaque judgment determines access and recourse without stable, inspectable criteria."
is:
  - "Case outcomes vary significantly by reviewer, shift, or channel with weak explanation quality."
  - "Policy language delegates broad exceptions without guardrails or appeal transparency."
isNot:
  - "Professional judgment bounded by explicit standards, calibration routines, and publishable reason codes."
  - "Temporary emergency powers with clear sunset and oversight."
predictions:
  - "People learn to optimize for who reviews rather than for what the rules say."
  - "Appeal success hinges on persistence and social fluency, not factual correction alone."
  - "Institutions cannot explain variation without blaming individual staff."
tests:
  - "Sample matched cases across reviewers and compare decision variance."
  - "Require reason codes that are specific enough to contest; generic codes indicate gate opacity."
---

Discretion is unavoidable in complex systems. Extraction begins when discretion is unbounded, unlogged, and effectively unappealable.
