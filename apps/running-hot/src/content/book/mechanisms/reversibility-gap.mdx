---
slug: reversibility-gap
status: draft
title: "Reversibility gap"
definition: "A structural asymmetry where institutions can impose harmful states quickly but cannot restore people to baseline at comparable speed."
is:
  - "Instant suspensions, cancellations, or denials paired with slow restoration channels."
  - "Interim harm accumulates while correction is pending."
isNot:
  - "Bi-directional controls where rollback and restoration are first-class capabilities."
  - "A brief hold with automatic safeguards preserving baseline rights."
predictions:
  - "Small error rates produce large downstream damage due to slow undo pathways."
  - "People avoid contesting wrong decisions because restoration lag is too costly."
  - "Staff rely on apologies and goodwill in place of formal rollback tools."
tests:
  - "Compare time-to-harm with time-to-repair; large ratios indicate a reversibility gap."
  - "Check whether benefits and access can be provisionally restored during review."
---

Justice depends on reversibility. If a system can break faster than it can repair, it will externalize risk by default.
