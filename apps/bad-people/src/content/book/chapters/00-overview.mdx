## What this book is for

When harm keeps happening, the usual question is “who is evil?” This book asks a different one: which system keeps causing it? It works through four steps you can use on any situation: spot the pattern, set the blame aside, name the mechanism, choose a next action.

## The four steps

### Recognition

Harm can continue even when everyone involved says they meant well. The first step is to name the pattern that keeps recurring.

### Exoneration

Separate personal character from system incentives. The harm is structural, not the work of a single bad actor.

### Mechanism

Six mechanisms explain most of it, and they show up in many settings: [transfer success](/read/mechanisms/transfer-success), [bureaucracy as moral technology](/read/mechanisms/bureaucracy-moral-technology), the [compliance trap](/read/mechanisms/compliance-trap), [delay as force](/read/mechanisms/delay-as-force), the [reputation membrane](/read/mechanisms/reputation-membrane), and [reversal capacity](/read/mechanisms/reversal-capacity).

### Agency

Each mechanism comes with an action you can take in ten minutes or less.

## Reader habits to use right away

- Follow the load: when harm appears, ask who is carrying the risk and cleanup work.
- Separate delay from repair: "we're reviewing it" is only real repair if a timeline and owner are clear.
- Count the true cost of recourse: include time, money, repeated effort, and emotional energy.
- Spot hidden buffering: ask who is quietly holding things together and what missing process is forcing that.
- Ask where stability costs land: don't just ask whether a system stayed up; ask who paid the price.
- Check whether friction protects or extracts: some friction is safety, some is just strain shifted onto people.
