## Volatility inputs

Sudden surges, ambiguous rules, and operational errors are normal friction. They are not rare faults. When an institution hits capacity, it must route this shock. If it meets a surge with funded backup and restores baseline, the system holds. If it routes that same spike into waiting and paperwork, the institution is externalizing pressure onto people.

## Load paths

When cooling capacity is underbuilt, strain is routed into human bodies. It moves toward the path of least resistance. That path is usually a person.

The strain lands in waiting time. It lands in the cognitive load of deciphering opaque rules. It lands in the physiological stress of repeated appeals. This happens because the system lacks the slack to absorb the load, not because anyone chose this path.

## Buffers vs extraction

A healthy system absorbs volatility with funded slack. It uses money, time, and redundancy to smooth out spikes. A failing system extracts that stability from people. It turns human patience into a resource. It treats human endurance as free infrastructure. This is the **crumple zone**.

When you are asked to "be patient" while the system processes your case, you may be acting as a buffer. You are absorbing the heat the institution refused to cool. This counts as **delay as force** only when the wait has no repair clock—no named owner, deadline, or remedy—and creates new harm. Bounded, accountable waits do not count.

## Ownership under failure

Strain lands hardest where ownership is vague. If no one is named as the owner of the repair, the burden defaults to the person experiencing the harm. They must find the right form, the right contact, and the right time.

If a missed callback sends someone back to the start, that is **attrition by queue**. It removes people from service pathways through structure, not merit. Without a named owner, deadline, and remedy, the waiting and the restarts fall on the claimant.

## Apply this checklist

- **Track the lag:** Does delay cluster at human interfaces rather than machine constraints?
- **Flag the restarts:** Are queue resets frequent enough to act as harm events?
- **Check the clock:** Does the delay have a repair clock with an owner and deadline?
- **Test the backup:** If the same spike hit with staffed backup, would the same people still absorb it?
