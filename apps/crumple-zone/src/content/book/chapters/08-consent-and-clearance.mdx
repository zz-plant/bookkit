## Claim

Blocked exit and permissioned re-entry are the same control. Both externalize the cost of stability onto the person.

The system keeps its stability by making exit costly and re-entry difficult. It forces you to absorb the friction of compliance. It treats your presence as a guarantee and your deviation as a debt. You pay for its continuity. You pay for its correction.

## Consent Maintenance Pressure

Genuine consent requires the ability to withdraw it. Without exit, there is only compliance.

Social, economic, and institutional pressures make withdrawal costly or blocked. The system treats initial consent as permanent. It assumes you will stay. It builds its architecture on that assumption. It removes the off-ramp.

Social pressure enforces this. You are told that leaving is a betrayal. You are told that you owe your continued presence.

Economic pressure enforces this. Your rent, your loan, your salary, your career path. These make exit costly.

Institutional pressure enforces this. The bureaucracy of staying is easier than the bureaucracy of leaving. The path of least resistance is compliance.

The right to exit is more important than the right to enter. Entry is a moment. Exit is a structure.

If you cannot leave, you are not a participant. You are a resource. The system takes your time and your attention to keep its own operations running smoothly.

Apply this check to your current commitments:

*   Does exit cost no more than entry?
*   Where have you felt unable to withdraw consent?
*   What made exit impossible or costly?

## Clearance Culture

When you deviate, you must ask to return. This is clearance culture.

Deviation triggers a clearance process. The burden of re-entry falls entirely on the person who deviated. You must manage the process. You must document your compliance. You must apologize for the deviation.

A worker returns from unplanned leave. They must submit medical notes. They must schedule a compliance review. They must wait for approval. The system uses this delay to enforce quiet compliance. The worker absorbs the stress and lost income. The institution keeps its predictable schedule.

Clearance is ongoing punishment disguised as due process. It is not fair. It is not about rehabilitation. It is about maintaining the burden on the deviant.

Apply this check to your re-entry experiences:

*   Who files the paperwork?
*   Who sets the deadline?
*   Who bears the delay?
*   Does the institution carry the re-entry burden, or do you?
*   What would re-entry look like if the burden were on the institution? The institution would file the paperwork and keep to its own deadline.
