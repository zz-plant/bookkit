slug: human-heat-sink
status: draft
title: "Human heat sink"
definition: "A system-stability pattern where institutions preserve output by exporting volatility into people's unpaid labor, delay exposure, and physiological stress."
is:
  - "The organization remains operational by routing spikes into waiting, paperwork, escalation friction, and emotional regulation demands on individuals."
  - "Cooling capacity (buffers, slack, rollback, coverage) is underbuilt while throughput targets remain unchanged."
isNot:
  - "A temporary busy period where the institution activates funded surge capacity and restores baseline safely."
  - "An individual coping issue explained only by mindset or resilience."
predictions:
  - "When volatility rises, queue times and administrative burden increase faster for lower-power participants."
  - "'Self-care' guidance appears without corresponding investment in staffing, redundancy, or recourse pathways."
  - "People with weaker documentation or advocacy capacity experience more heat injuries (missed care, churn, attrition, debt, conflict)."
tests:
  - "Ask: if the same demand spike happened with staffed backup, explicit rollback, and clear pager ownership, would the same people still burn?"
  - "Track where delays accumulate; if lag clusters at human interfaces rather than machine constraints, heat is being socialized."
---

Systems run hot either by design or neglect. If they do not dissipate heat in infrastructure, they dissipate it in humans.
